### Step 1: the two named devices (committed)

**Multiband delay.** `BandDelayParams` gained a layout choice (three bands as
before, or sixteen), an active bank (1 to 8) and eight banks of sixteen delay,
level and pan values: 386 parameters appended after the existing fifteen, so
saved indices 0 to 14 are unchanged and a project saved before this opens as
the three-band layout with every new field at its default. The three-band
path runs the same arithmetic as before. The sixteen-band path splits at
fifteen fixed edges from 40 Hz to 16 kHz in equal ratios (Windfall's choice:
the public definition gives no band frequencies), each band the difference of
two neighbouring two-section one-pole lowpasses so the bands still sum to the
input. Its bands share the three feedback controls by where their centre falls
against the two crossover controls. A layout change fades the echoes out over
5 ms, clears them and fades the new layout in.

Import: every `bank_N/{delay,vol,pan}.M` float lands on
`banks.{N-1}.{timeMs,level,pan}.M`. Scaling: the raw float is taken in the
stand-in's own unit (milliseconds, gain, pan) and clamped to its range
(1..1000, 0..1, -1..1), because the public definition names the arrays and
gives no unit. The layout is set to sixteen bands and bank 1 plays, because
the public definition names no playing bank; feedback and mix stay at the
stand-in defaults because it names neither. The report keeps one line per bank
and row with every source name, value and target.

One existing expectation changed, in `round6_native.rs`: it read bank 1 band 0
from `timeMs.0`, the legacy "low" control of the three-band layout. That
control is not what the field describes; it now reads `banks.0.timeMs.0`. The
value asserted (120) is unchanged.

**Three-oscillator synth (SimSynth).** `TripleOscParams` gained, appended after
index 19: a pulse width and two octave-layer switches per oscillator, and the
level of the filter's band-pass and high-pass outputs. With the new fields at
their defaults the output is sample-identical to before (tested against a
project saved in the old shape).

- `osc*_pw`: DawVert `wave.py` draws its square high where the sine exceeds
  `(pw - 0.5) * 2`, which is a share `0.5 - asin((pw - 0.5) * 2) / pi` of the
  cycle. That share is the stand-in's pulse width; a square with a width other
  than half becomes the pulse waveform. Other shapes carry the value unheard,
  as in the public converter.
- `osc*_o1`, `osc*_o2`: DawVert adds the same wave at twice and four times the
  pitch, each mixed half and half with what is already there. The two switches
  do exactly that.
- `svf_band`, `svf_high`: no public rule; the 0..1 value is the level of that
  filter output beside the low-pass (band at unity peak gain). A switched-off
  source filter sets both to 0.
- `lfo_del`, `lfo_retrigger`: the channel LFOs gained a start delay and a
  retrigger switch (`TargetLfo.delay_ms`, `.retrigger`, both defaulted for old
  projects; the engine holds the wave out for the delay and fades it in over
  5 ms; retriggered, the wave waits at the start of its cycle). The public
  converter reads `lfo_del` without giving it a rule, so the delay uses the
  time rule the same device publishes for its envelopes, `4 * v^3` seconds.
  The channel-level LFO's public retrigger flag (PyFLP `LFOPhaseRetrig`,
  bit 5) now maps to the same switch.

Two expectations changed for the new fields: the parameter counts in
`analog/tests.rs` (4 to 7 and 20 to 31, with the old indices asserted
unchanged), and one whole-object comparison of a default LFO in
`voice-panel.test.tsx`, which now lists the two new fields at their defaults.
Fixture literals that build a `TargetLfo` field by field gained a default tail.

## Row `wf-project-settings`

### What was wrong

Per-note pan and volume reached a hosted plugin only when it offered note
instances, pan and volume together. A plugin with pan and no volume got the
plugin's own pan and no law; one with neither got nothing; nothing was ever
applied after the plugin; and the capability check refused the project law
outright (`require_pan_law`). When a plugin changed what it offers, the unit
that replaces it started each replayed note at its target value, and the old
unit went silent in one step.

### What changed

The rule from `docs/qa/README.md` is now the code
(`crates/windfall-plugin-host/src/project_expression.rs`, shared by the
in-process owner and the helper process):

- Each of pan and volume has one route. The plugin applies it per note where
  it offers that on the note's channel; the channel applies it to the whole
  output after the plugin where it does not. One plugin has one output, so a
  property the channel must apply for any sounding note is applied by the
  channel for all of them, and the channel follows the most recently started
  sounding note.
- `NoteRamp::split` divides one note's project-law gain between the two.
  With the law's gains `(l, r)` and the note's volume `v`, a plugin that pans
  `(1 - p, p)` needs `p = r / (l + r)` and volume `v (l + r) / 2`. With a
  weight per property (1 in the plugin, 0 on the channel) the plugin is sent
  `1/2 + wp (p - 1/2)` and `1 + wv (volume - 1)` and the channel makes up
  exactly the rest, so the product is the law's gain at any weight. No
  combination applies the law twice or not at all.
- A route change moves the weights over 5 ms from the next frame of a block
  boundary. The ramp's value, target and remaining frames are untouched. A
  property the plugin stops offering goes to the channel at once (the plugin
  stopped applying it at once, so that is what keeps the product).
- The channel's share is delayed by the plugin's reported latency, so the two
  halves meet on the same output frame.
- A capability change replaces the hosted unit. Before the plugin leaves, the
  output fades out over 5 ms and the notes' ramps wait; the unit writes each
  sounding note (engine identity, key, channel, ramp) to a carry shared per
  plugin target. The replacement finds a replayed note by its identity,
  continues its ramp where it stopped, moves the routes to its own
  capabilities and fades in. A plugin without note instances is played by key
  and channel as before, and its notes are now owned so the channel can apply
  their pan and volume.
- The helper process applies the same rule and channel stage to what its
  plugin returns, and a processor reset inside the helper no longer restarts
  the ramps of notes the snapshot names again.
- `require_pan_law` and `require_expression` refuse only an invalid channel.
  The capability panel shows, per MIDI channel, "In the plugin, per note" or
  "On the channel, after the plugin" for pan and for volume, and when either
  is on the channel explains that it applies to the whole output and follows
  the most recent note. The old sentence that the law "cannot" be applied is
  gone, with its catalog entries.

Fixture: VST3 class 27, "VST3 Volume Expression Probe" (volume per note, no
pan), appended; the class count assertions moved from 27 to 28.

Expectations rewritten because they asserted the ruled-out behaviour, each
with a comment saying so: `note_capabilities::tests` (was "requires all three
capabilities"), `ownership_tests::vst3_missing_native_volume_...` (was a
refusal with unchanged PCM; now the law's gain on the channel), and three

### Still open on this row

- **Bridged hosting does not yet carry a ramp or fade across a capability
  change.** There the change replaces the helper process, and the new helper
  starts each replayed note at its target. The route split, channel stage,
  latency alignment and ramp preservation across a reset inside one helper
  are done and measured above; carrying the ramp across helper processes
  needs the note snapshot of the bridge protocol to hold the ramp (start
  values, elapsed frames, weights), which is a protocol version change.
- **A bridged plugin without note instances** is still played without its
  notes' pan and volume: the helper is sent key and channel only. In process
  this case is handled.
- No fixture changes a capability without being replaced, so the same-unit
  path (routes re-read at a block boundary, a lost property handed to the
  channel at once) is covered by the shared unit tests and not by a native
  fixture.
